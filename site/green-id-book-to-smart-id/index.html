Switching a green ID book to a Smart ID

In short. Switching a South African green ID book to a Smart ID: whether it is still valid, the dates announced for 2027 and 2028, the fee, and which banks take it.

This page is for someone who still holds a green barcoded identity book and wants a Smart ID card. It sets out what the Department of Home Affairs (DHA), gov.za and each bank say about whether that book is still valid, the dates a minister has announced, what happens to the book, the fee, the documents, and which banks name this switch. A Smart ID card that was lost, stolen or damaged is a different application: Replacing a lost or stolen Smart ID. A first identity document, including at 16, for someone who has never held one: First Smart ID at 16. The tariff table is on Fees and documents. How a bank visit differs from a Home Affairs office booking: Booking and walk-ins.

The end dates are in a speech. They are not on the current application pages.

On 1 October 2026 the Minister of Home Affairs announced that production of the green ID book would cease by 31 March 2027, and that on 31 March 2028 the book would cease to be recognised [source]. A Department statement on 26 October 2026 says production will cease on 31 March 2027, and that recognition will be withdrawn by 31 March 2028 [source]. Those are not the same words as the speech. The current identity documents page and the Digital Partnership page do not name either date [source, source]. The Identification Amendment Act says a green bar-coded identity document remains valid until it is replaced, or until a date the Minister fixes by notice in the Government Gazette, whichever is sooner [source]. The speech and the 26 October statement do not say that a Gazette notice fixing 31 March 2028 has already been published. The identity documents page and the Digital Partnership page do not say that either [source, source, source, source].

What the Act and the 2013 notice say about validity

The Identification Amendment Act 28 of 2000 substituted section 25 of the Identification Act, 1997. It commenced on 13 October 2000. In the text on gov.za [source]:

  • Subsection (2): a green, bar-coded identity document issued under the Identification Act, 1986 remains valid until it is replaced by an identity card issued under section 14, or until a date contemplated in subsection (4), whichever is sooner.
  • Subsection (4): the Minister may by notice in the Gazette fix a date for that replacement, and may make regulations about the replacement.
  • Subsection (1) is one sentence. It says the Director-General continues to issue green, bar-coded identity documents until a date the Minister determines by notice in the Gazette. That sentence has no paragraph (b).

The 1 October 2026 speech cites section 25(1)(b) of the Identification Act for the 31 March 2028 date [source]. The amendment text published on gov.za does not contain section 25(1)(b) [source]. This page does not choose between the speech and the Act text.

Government Notice 728 of 2013, in Government Gazette 36648 of 11 July 2013, is the notice on replacing green, bar-coded identity documents with identity cards. It takes effect on 18 July 2013. On validity, it says [source]:

  • When the identity card is issued, the green bar-coded identity document becomes invalid from the time of issue, if the person had one. The person must then surrender it to the Department for cancellation.
  • At the in-person step, the person submits the green identity document for cancellation, provided that it is not surrendered until the identity card is handed over.
  • A green bar-coded identity document that the Department has not cancelled, where the person has not been issued with an identity card, remains valid during the roll-out until a date fixed by notice in the Gazette.
  • The notice says that once the programme has been rolled out, and at least within three years from its effective date, or soon thereafter, the Minister shall by notice in the Gazette fix a date, and on that date all green bar-coded identity documents shall cease to be valid.

That notice does not fix 31 March 2027 or 31 March 2028. Three years from 18 July 2013 is not a date the notice prints. The current identity documents page and the Digital Partnership page do not repeat the surrender sentence [source, source].

The dates in the 1 October 2026 speech

  • DHA will cease production of the green ID book by 31 March 2027. On that date the machines that produce the booklet will be permanently shut down and decommissioned [source].
  • Before 31 March 2027, the Department will enable the remaining naturalised citizens and permanent residents, and South African citizens abroad, to obtain Smart IDs as well. The speech describes these groups as historically excluded [source].
  • After production ends in March 2027, the 14.7 million South African citizens and permanent residents who still hold only a green ID book will have a further 12 months to switch [source].
  • On 31 March 2028, the speech says, the green ID book will officially cease to be recognised as a valid form of identification, in terms of section 25(1)(b) of the Identification Act [source].
  • First-time applicants, and applicants over 60, will remain exempt from the application fee. The fee section below is where the other official pages do, and do not, say the same thing [source].
  • The speech says the switch at a local bank branch takes as little as five minutes, and that this service needs no prior booking and no paperwork [source].

The Digital Partnership page, checked for this guide, still says the time in the branch differs by bank and is approximately 15 minutes, and that you do not need to bring any documents [source]. It does not use the five-minute figure. Capitec says the visit normally takes a few minutes. FNB says the in-branch process takes about 10 minutes [source, source]. Use the figure on the page for the place you are going.

Branch totals in the older statements are those statements' totals: 167 in the 15 May 2026 speech, at eight weeks after the new system went live, and 372 on 3 August 2026 [source, source]. They are not a current directory. The live locator, and the lists built from it, are on the home page and the province pages.

Who can do this switch at a bank

The Digital Partnership page lists green ID book holders converting to a Smart ID card as a service that is currently available. Under who can apply, the same page says the first phase focuses on South African citizens who still hold the green ID book, re-issues for existing Smart ID card holders, and naturalised citizens and qualifying permanent residents with green ID books [source].

A second list on that page, under who the service is currently available for, names permanent residents and naturalised citizens from visa-exempt countries, in the initial phase [source]. "Qualifying permanent residents with green ID books" and "from visa-exempt countries" are not the same words. The page does not explain the difference.

The 1 October 2026 speech says the Department will enable the remaining naturalised citizens and permanent residents, and citizens abroad, before 31 March 2027 [source]. "Will enable" is the speech's tense. It does not say that every permanent resident, or every citizen abroad, can already apply at a bank on the day of this check.

People living abroad: the identity documents page says they can apply for ID books at a South African mission or consulate, and that the application is sent to Head Office in Pretoria. A Smart ID card at a mission: not stated in that sentence [source].

Children cannot apply at these bank branches. A first ID, and the example the Digital Partnership page gives is turning 16, must be done at a Home Affairs office [source]. That application is on First Smart ID at 16.

Someone else applying for you: the Digital Partnership page says no. You attend in person so that fingerprints and a photograph can be captured [source]. If the branch cannot process the application, that page says you may be referred to a Home Affairs office [source].

What these sources do not state

  • That 31 March 2028 is already printed in a Gazette notice. Section 25(4) allows the Minister to fix a date by notice in the Gazette. The 1 October 2026 speech announces the date and cites section 25(1)(b). It does not say the notice has been published. The 26 October 2026 statement repeats a recognition date and does not say the notice has been published. Notice 728 of 2013 does not fix that date [source, source, source, source].
  • That the identity documents page or the Digital Partnership page has adopted the 2027 and 2028 dates [source, source].
  • That the 20 November 2017 statement, which rejected a 31 March 2018 end date, is the rule the 2026 speeches are applying [source, source].
  • That every bank waives the fee for a person of 60 or older who is switching from a green ID book. The identity documents page, the brochure and the 1 October 2026 speech state an exemption. Capitec, Standard Bank, Absa and Nedbank do not state it. FNB states it for a first Smart ID [source, source, source, source, source, source, source, source].
  • That Absa's renewal page, or FNB's no-appointment replacement, is the green ID book conversion. Those banks do not use those words. DHA's partnership page does name the conversion as a branch service [source, source, source].
  • One document list that both a Home Affairs office and a bank will follow [source, source, source, source].

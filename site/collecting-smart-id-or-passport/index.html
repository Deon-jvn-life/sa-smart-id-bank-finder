Collecting a Smart ID or passport

This page is for the step after you have applied: how the Department of Home Affairs (DHA) or the bank tells you a Smart ID or passport is ready, where and when you collect it, what to bring, who may collect it, and any deadline a source states. How to apply is on Booking and walk-ins. Tariffs and the application document lists are on Fees and documents. A card that was lost or stolen: Replacing a lost or stolen Smart ID. A first identity document from age 16: First Smart ID at 16. Switching from a green ID book, including whether that book is still valid: Green ID book to Smart ID.

Do not copy one bank's collection rule onto another route.

The Digital Partnership page says you collect at the bank branch where the application was made, and that the partner bank communicates the collection details. Delivery is not in that answer [source]. The partner-branch page says you collect from your selected branch, or have the document delivered where the bank offers that [source]. Only Standard Bank states that you must collect your own ID, and only Standard Bank states a 12 month collection window [source]. Where another source is silent, this page says so.

At a Home Affairs office

  • A green barcoded ID book can only be collected from the office where the application was made [source].
  • A Smart ID card is collected at the preferred modernised office you chose when you applied. Modernised offices, on that page, include banks, DHA offices in malls, and mobile offices [source].
  • Specify the office of collection before the application transaction is closed or submitted. The page prints that as a warning [source].
  • DHA uses the cellphone number you provided during the application to send updates about progress and the readiness for collection [source].
  • The word SMS, an email, how many messages, and what the message says: not stated by source [source].
  • What to bring to that collection, who may collect for you, a fee for collecting, and a deadline after which the document is sent away or destroyed: not stated by source [source].

People living abroad can apply for ID books at a South African mission or consulate. Applications are sent to Head Office in Pretoria, and delivery takes longer than a local application because of the logistics. That sentence is about ID books. A Smart ID card collected at a mission: not stated in that sentence [source].

Public office hours on the DHA contacts page: Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 15:30. Every last Wednesday of the month, 9:00 to 15:30. The page calls these the hours offices are open to the public. A collection-only counter, or a different collection time: not stated on that page. The enquiry number printed there is 0800 60 11 90 [source].

A passport, as distinct from the Smart ID

  • The travel documents page gives issuance turnaround, not a collection appointment and not a destruction deadline: 13 working days for an ordinary tourist passport and for a maxi passport, and 18 working days for a minor tourist passport. Where you collect the passport: not stated on that page [source].
  • On that page, an application inside South Africa can be submitted at a Home Affairs front office with live capture, at participating banks, or through eHomeAffairs. The application must be made in person. Collection in person, and collection by someone else: not stated by source [source].
  • The Digital Partnership page still lists a passport application during phase 1 as not yet available at those bank branches [source].
  • The partner-branch page lists an adult standard or maxi passport, from age 18, for citizens and for naturalised citizens, and its collect-or-deliver step is the step quoted above [source].
  • A gov.za statement of 9 March 2026 says that throughout 2026 the Digital Partnership will also enable passport services and courier delivery [source].
  • A gov.za statement of 22 July 2026 says the partnership will continue to expand before the end of the year to include adult and minor passports, first-time Smart ID card applications, and, as the next major milestone, home delivery of identity documents to citizens' homes [source].
  • A gov.za statement of 26 October 2026 says the Department will shortly roll out passport, first-time Smart ID, and home delivery services through these bank branches. The same statement says that within a matter of months, five-minute applications for Smart ID and passport services, with the option of delivery to a doorstep, will be available at over one thousand bank branches [source]. Whether any bank already offers that delivery: not stated by source.
  • This page was checked on 26 October 2026. On that date the Digital Partnership page still listed phase 1 passports as not yet available, and the partner-branch page already listed adult passports and delivery where the bank offers it. Neither page explains the difference [source, source].
  • Standard Bank's online route names first-time passport issuing and passport renewal, an SMS from Home Affairs, and collection at the selected branch [source]. FNB's eHomeAffairs collect step names the Smart ID or the passport, and original identity documents [source]. Capitec's two articles, and Absa's Smart ID page: a passport collection: not stated by source [source, source, source].

Older statements about a deadline

A Home Affairs media briefing dated 19 August 2011 said that, at the end of July 2011, 610 655 IDs were still to be collected, and that a further 269 940 IDs were awaiting destruction at Head Office after applicants had not collected them for twelve months. It told people to collect from the office of application. Those figures are the briefing's figures for July 2011. The current identity documents page and the Digital Partnership page do not state a 12 month rule, and they do not repeat those counts [source, source, source].

Standard Bank's 12 month answer is the only current bank page in this set that states a collection window, and it states it for the ID [source].

A DHA statement published on 4 December 2025 extended office hours on 8 to 19 December 2025 and 5 to 16 January 2026, by two hours a day, for applications and collections. On those days, offices equipped for Smart ID cards and passports would close at 18:00 instead of 16:00. Clients were advised to book an application or collection appointment on the Branch Appointment Booking System (BABS) before visiting. People without a booking could still visit as walk-ins. The statement says dedicated counters are offered for collections, in that message to people who had applied for Smart ID cards and passports. Whether those hours, that booking advice, or those counters apply on other dates: not stated by source [source]. The booking routes are on Booking and walk-ins.

What these sources do not state

  • One collection rule for every bank. Absa's page does not state collection at all [source].
  • That the Standard Bank 12 month window, or the duty to collect your own ID, applies at Capitec, FNB, Absa, Nedbank, or a Home Affairs office [source, source, source].
  • A fee charged for the collection visit itself [source, source, source].
  • Which banks, on 26 October 2026, actually offer the delivery the partner-branch page allows [source, source].
  • The screen or number you use to ask for a status if no message arrives. The contacts page and FNB print 0800 60 11 90. Standard Bank prints 0800 601 190 and says it does not control collection dates [source, source, source].

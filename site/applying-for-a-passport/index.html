Applying for a South African passport

This page is for a passport application: where the Department of Home Affairs (DHA) says you can lodge it, which bank page names a passport, what to carry, who must attend for a child, and which fee each official page prints. It does not repeat the tariff table. That table is on Fees and documents. How to book a Home Affairs office, and how a bank visit differs, is on Booking and walk-ins. Notice, place, and any deadline after the passport is ready: Collecting a Smart ID or passport. A first identity document, which a person of 16 or older needs before a passport: First Smart ID at 16.

Official pages do not agree on whether a bank can take a passport today.

The Digital Partnership page lists a passport application during phase 1 as not yet available, and it says clients who need that service must visit a Home Affairs office [source]. The partner-branch page says DHA now offers adult tourist passport applications at participating bank branches, and that the passport service is currently available [source]. A gov.za statement of 26 October 2026 says the Department will shortly roll out passport services through these bank branches [source]. "Will shortly" and "currently available" are not the same tense. This page was checked on 26 October 2026, and it does not choose a tense for you.

Where an application can be lodged

South African passports and travel documents are issued in terms of the South African Passports and Travel Documents Act, No. 4 of 1994, and the related regulations. The travel documents page says an application inside South Africa can be submitted at any Home Affairs front office with live capture (it also says a Smart ID card office), at participating banks, or through eHomeAffairs. For the banks, that page says to refer to the DHA website for a list. An application outside the country goes to the nearest South African embassy or consulate [source].

  • The application must be made in person, whether it is lodged in South Africa or abroad [source].
  • Applications lodged outside South Africa are estimated to take at least six to eight months, because of the logistics [source].
  • The gov.za service page says you need a passport to travel abroad, and that you must have a South African identity document, or a birth certificate if you are a child. It points to the Branch Appointment Booking System (BABS) office list, and it tells you to book at services.dha.gov.za. What that booking address says, and how BABS differs from a bank visit: Booking and walk-ins [source, source].
  • The BABS notice says the services on that booking system are for smart ID cards and passports [source].
  • DHA's eHomeAffairs how-to is the sequence for a Smart ID card and/or passport application that can end at a bank office. Those steps are on Booking and walk-ins [source].

  • A person aged 16 or older must first apply for an identity document, so that fingerprints can be registered, before applying for a passport. If you do not have a South African identity number, you must first apply for registration of your birth and for an identity document, as applicable [source]. That identity document is on First Smart ID at 16.
  • The partner-branch passport list starts at 18, for a standard or maxi passport, for citizens and for naturalised citizens. It does not say that "standard" is the 32-page ordinary passport. A minor passport, and ages 16 and 17: not in that list [source].
  • Passports are issued with the names as they appear in the National Population Register (NPR) at the time of application. Any change of name must be applied for, finalised, and recorded in the NPR before the passport application is submitted [source]. Name changes and amendments to personal details are not yet available at Digital Partnership branches. Those clients must visit a Home Affairs office [source]. Standard Bank says it cannot assist with a surname change [source].
  • In terms of section 26(b) of the South African Citizenship Act, 1995, the travel documents page says it is a punishable offence for a South African citizen aged 18 or older to leave or enter South Africa using a foreign passport. The penalty: not stated on that page [source].
  • The passport must be valid for at least 30 days after the intended date of departure, and it must contain at least two blank pages for entry stamps. Those pages must be facing each other, and they cannot include the endorsement page at the back. If a visa is required, the page says one blank page is for the visa and one is for the entry stamp [source].

A child, and consent under 18

Parents and children must each complete a separate application form. Both parents and the children must be present when applying for passports for children. The page says to see the exceptions under tourist passports for persons under 16. Both parents must have their identity documents, and an original or a certified copy of the child's birth certificate [source].

The same general section also says [source]:

  • If one parent is unavailable, and both parents' details are on the birth certificate, there must be a consent or an affidavit, obtainable from a Home Affairs office or from an embassy abroad.
  • If the parents are divorced or separated, the parent applying needs proof of full custody. If there is a dispute about custody, that parent must seek consent through the High Court.
  • If a parent cannot be located, or refuses to consent, or a dispute about consent arises, the matter goes to the Children's Court. The court's decision must be submitted with the application.
  • If one parent has died, a copy of the death certificate is required.

The later section, for persons 15 or younger, is more specific, and it does not say that it replaces the general section. Both are on the same page [source].

For a minor tourist passport, persons 15 or younger, the travel documents page says [source]:

  • A completed passport application form, obtainable from a Home Affairs office. The form number in this section: not stated. The adult sections name DHA-73.
  • If the parents are married, both parents and the child sign, and both parents should be in attendance.
  • The child and both parents should attend when the application is submitted at the nearest Home Affairs office or South African mission or consulate.
  • If one parent cannot attend the same office, that parent must visit their nearest Home Affairs office with their original identity document. The application remains pending until the second parent provides consent in person. A note under that rule says a letter of consent and an ID copy are no longer accepted.
  • If the parents are divorced, and sole parental rights and responsibilities concerning guardianship have not been granted to one parent, the child and both parents should attend, and both must sign.
  • If a parent has died, the death certificate and a copy must accompany the application.
  • For a minor born out of wedlock, the biological father's consent is also required if any of the circumstances in section 21 of the Children's Act, 1995, apply, and the child and both parents should attend. What those circumstances are: not printed on this page.
  • If a guardian other than the parents has care of the child, proof of the High Court's appointment must accompany the application.
  • If a parent cannot be located, refuses, or a dispute arises, the Children's Court decision must be submitted with the application. This repeats the general section.
  • The child's South African birth certificate and a copy.
  • Two colour passport photographs, not needed where the process is automated.
  • The prescribed fee of R600, which is the figure in this list, not the six-month figure in the type section.
  • A parental consent letter can be downloaded from the page. The link in that sentence goes to a PDF on dha.gov.za [source].

FNB, for a minor on the eHomeAffairs route: both parents must accompany the child for a passport, and one parent for a Smart ID. The age of that minor: not stated by source [source].

The Digital Partnership page says children cannot apply at those branches [source]. The partner-branch passport list starts at 18 [source].

The general section's consent or affidavit, and the later note that a letter of consent and an ID copy are no longer accepted, are both printed. The page does not say which sentence an office will follow if you arrive with only a letter [source].

Temporary passports, and an emergency certificate

DHA's notice, and the travel documents page, say temporary passports are no longer issued, from 1 September 2014. The notice gives the reasons as refusals of entry on a temporary passport, a shorter turnaround for the 10-year passport, and abuse. The travel documents page states the date and points to that notice [source, source].

Both pages say an emergency travel certificate (ETC) is what can be issued instead, and only for emergency travel [source, source]:

  • The passport is lost or stolen while the person is abroad.
  • Or the person is travelling to a country that accepts an ETC in an emergency, and the examples given are a family member in distress who needs immediate assistance, or a death in the family.

The travel documents page adds a limit the notice does not: you can only travel from the country where the ETC is issued to South Africa, which that page calls the country of origin [source]. The notice does not print that return-only sentence [source].

The only ETC amount on the fees page is R140.00, and it sits in the table of fees at missions. An ETC fee inside South Africa: not stated in that table [source].

The fees page still lists, at R600, an official passport (including an official emergency passport) or a crew member certificate, and a temporary passport issued for official purposes. It does not say that temporary-passport line has been withdrawn [source].

- 25 October 2026: shipped **Switching a green ID book to a Smart ID** at `green-id-book-to-smart-id/`. Chosen over a Johannesburg page. A city page would still be a filter of the province list: DHA publishes no Johannesburg-only fee, hour or document rule, and the Gauteng page already lists every locator row. The green ID book is the main bank service, and no page had set out whether the book is still valid. The 1 October 2026 Quantum Leap speech names 31 March 2027 for the end of production and 31 March 2028 for the end of recognition, and it cites section 25(1)(b). The Identification Amendment Act 28 of 2000, as published on gov.za, has no section 25(1)(b): section 25(2) keeps the book valid until it is replaced or until a Gazette date under section 25(4), whichever is sooner. Notice 728 of 2013 says the book becomes invalid when the card is issued and must be surrendered then, not before, and it does not fix 2028. The 20 November 2017 statement rejected a rumoured 31 March 2018 end date. The 15 May 2026 budget vote and the 3 August 2026 locator statement encourage the switch and do not name 2027 or 2028. The identity documents page says a green-book conversion is a re-issue at R140, and free at 60 and older. The August 2026 fees table does not put that 60-and-older exemption in the re-issue line. The Digital Partnership page offers the conversion, says no documents, and states R140 with no 60-and-older exemption. Capitec and Standard Bank name the green book. FNB's no-appointment replacement and Absa's renewal page do not. Nedbank says the card replaces the barcoded book and says to bring the ID book to the appointment. Sources: dha.gov.za identity documents page, fees page, Digital Partnership page, and Smart ID requirements brochure; gov.za Identification Amendment Act 28 of 2000, Notice 728 of 2013, the 20 November 2017 statement, the 15 May 2026 budget vote, the 3 August 2026 locator statement, the 1 October 2026 Quantum Leap speech, and the affidavit FAQ; myhomeaffairsonline.dha.gov.za partner-branch page (Absa is in the locator); Capitec, Standard Bank, FNB, Absa and Nedbank Smart ID pages.

Chosen over a city page, a payment-only page, and a per-bank repeat. Johannesburg still has no official fee, hour or document rule of its own. The Digital Partnership page says name changes and amendments to personal details are not yet available at bank branches. Standard Bank says it cannot do the surname change, and it does list a Smart ID replacement due to marriage or divorce after Home Affairs has already changed the surname. Capitec, FNB, Absa and Nedbank do not name a forename or surname change. The gov.za identity-document page and the identity documents page describe an ID re-issue with a marriage and do not mention section 26. The amendments page writes the witness-protection citation as Act No. 112 of 198. The regulation writes Act No. 112 of 1998. The 1992 Act PDF still defines a person of age as 21. Act 1 of 2002 substitutes 18. Section 27 requires Gazette publication for a person of age under sections 23, 24(1) and 26(2), with a 1998 exception for a witness protection plan. The amendments page states publication for a forename at 18 and older, and not in its section 26 block. A gov.za file of 8 November 2024 still publishes section 24 and section 26 alterations. A processing time is not stated.
